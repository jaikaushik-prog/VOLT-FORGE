# Recommendation

**Use 2 LG M50T cells in parallel, with the negative output at the far cell, for the Raspberry Pi 5 gateway backup.** It is the smallest qualifying design among the parallel arrangements tested.

This is a simulation result. Nothing here has been built or measured. Generated by `scripts/write_recommendation.py` from `reports/design_study_v2/run1/summary.json`, software `97ca199f2fd21aac`.

## The result

The mission is 25 W for 10 minutes through an assumed 90% converter, held at 8.42 A, with a 3.3 V floor at 23 °C. A design qualifies only if every required Pi case passes: nominal, mismatch at every cell position in both directions, thermal sensitivities and converter efficiency. ## Why 2 cells

- **One cell is refused.** At 8.42 A it exceeds the 7.275 A limit, in all 15 required cases.
- **2 cells pass every required case.** Worst-case current is 5.33 A, which leaves 1.95 A of margin, and the lowest voltage is 3.50 V.
- **It halves the cell purchase.** ₹4,824 to ₹2,412, and 280 g to 140 g. This is cells only, not a complete backup unit.
- **Temperature does not change the decision.** The worst 2-cell case reaches 31.6 °C, including halved cooling and heat capacity.

## What argues against it

- **It has less margin.** Four cells stay at 3.34 A in their worst required case, against 5.33 A here.
- **The 12 A stress test can fail.** 2 of 62 combined mismatch samples were refused for exceeding the cell limit. All 62 samples passed at the Pi load. Samples show sensitivity, not a probability.
- **No link option wins everywhere.** The opposite-end output gave the best worst-case margin, 5.33 A against 5.49 A. It still raised peak current in 35 of 81 paired cases. The cell count is the real decision, and the output position is a small refinement.
- **Temperatures are not validated.** All 12 pre-declared measured comparison cases missed their bands. Treat temperatures as a range.
- **The board fits, but its limits are unmeasured.** Geekworm sells a 2-cell Pi 5 board, the X1205: 5.1 V at up to 6 A, up to 95% efficiency and a 2.4 V cutoff on v1.1. It publishes no battery overcurrent threshold, and its cell layout is fixed, so the far-cell output needs your own wiring. The same-end layout also passes all 33 required cases, at 5.49 A worst case.
- **It has no spare.** The tests cover a weak cell, not a lost one. If one cell or its contact fails, one cell is left at a load it cannot carry. Three cells is the smallest design that survives losing a cell.
- **The load matters.** 25 W is the supply rating, not a measured draw. Below about 21.6 W one cell stays under its limit, so measure the Pi before buying.
- **It needs warmth.** Below 10 °C the cell limit falls to 2.425 A, so two cells could not carry this load. The study holds 23 °C.

## Checks on the decision

Frozen in `configs/engineering_checks_v1.json` before running. Results are in `reports/engineering_checks_v1/result.json`.

- **Is 8.42 A fair?** Yes, and it errs on the hard side. A true 25 W load peaks at 7.81 A, so the study overstates cell current by about 8%.
- **Current sharing against a real module.** This check missed. On test 28 each cell's share landed within 1.1 points on average, inside the 2.5 point band, but the busiest cell was named in only 62% of samples against a required 80%. An equal-share guess scores 2.38 points, so the band was generous. On test 53 the cells shared almost evenly and the model did no better than that guess. Shares cancel a gain common to all sensors, not a gain that differs by sensor.
- **Voltage error against margin.** In the charge range the Pi job uses, the largest error on test 28 was 0.17 V, always optimistic. The worst-case margin is 0.20 V, so it clears, but only by 0.03 V.
- **Heat error against the limit.** Real rises reached 2.27 times the simulated rise. Scaling the worst case by that gives 42.4 °C against the 55 °C limit. That 2.27 comes from one 2.5 A run taken close to empty. Over the first 10 minutes, the length of this job, the measured rise was 0.27 to 1.13 times the simulated rise.
- **OCV slope against entropy (VF3-08).** The worst variant reaches 35.0 °C, so the open question does not change this decision. The mismatch itself is still unresolved.
- **LG low-charge change.** The lowest charge across all 33 required cases is 54.0%, well above the 18% where the change applies.
- **Virtual protection.** With a 7 A, 3.3 V and 45 °C cutoff, the design ran the full job without tripping. Status: completed.
