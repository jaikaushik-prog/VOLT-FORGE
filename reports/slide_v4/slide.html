Problem understanding

Cells rarely share the load evenly

Say a Raspberry Pi 5 gateway must survive a 10-minute power cut at 25 W, its full supply rating, at 23 °C. Four cells in parallel sounds easy, until the cell closest to the output takes more current at first. Ageing and packaging come later.

Desirability

You shouldn't need a lab to learn that

Today that takes a bench, a paid tool or your own code. Students can't afford that and engineers can't wait. Here you wire the circuit on screen and see which cell struggles before spending a rupee.

Technical feasibility

So how few cells can do this job?

We compared 3 and 2 cells against the 4-cell start. Two cells held the Pi for all 10 minutes in all 33 simulated tests, even with one weak cell. One cell was refused. It should fit a two-cell Pi UPS board, though we haven't built it yet, and the tool runs on a laptop.

Engineering quality

Why should you trust it?

Every run must balance its energy, which catches solver errors, not a wrong model. Against published data, voltage and temperature missed all 12 checks. Current sharing came closer but also missed: the nearest cell took 34% early on, the model said 34%. Over 10 minutes, real heating stayed within 1.13 times the model.

Innovation

You don't have to guess

Press Find the smallest design. It rebuilds the circuit at each size and tests it under normal conditions. For the Pi: 2 cells, not 4, at half the cell cost. Our study then stress-tested that.
